## B1: keep interval inventory and use overlap

Keep both INTERVAL and AS_OF. An interval inventory means the selected episodes that overlap that window. It does not mean the subset whose occupants stayed for the whole window. Preserve distinct visits of the same occupant, overlapping episodes, exact identities/revisions, source order, and every full original interval. Do not collapse visits, trim the original facts to the query, sum quantities, or infer uninterrupted occupancy from their union.

Use the original inclusion flags when deciding overlap. For two finite intervals, the lower intersection bound is the later lower bound and the upper is the earlier upper bound. A tied bound is included only when both operands include it. An intersection exists if its lower bound precedes its upper bound, or they are equal and both resulting bounds include the point. Half-open intervals touching at an excluded boundary do not overlap; a common included point does. A degenerate included-point intersection must be representable as a point, rather than emitted as a schema-invalid zero-length interval.

Report the query intersection separately from the unchanged full source interval. Also distinguish the portion actually supported by the source's applicability. The observed support is bounded by the episode, the source applicability, the query and the observation horizon. If source applicability covers only part of a known query intersection, do not call the remaining part supported. A known episode spanning the whole window with narrower observed applicability must not receive full-window coverage.

Report full-window versus partial-window coverage explicitly. For AS_OF, require membership of the selected point, with the same inclusion rules. An episode overlapping an INTERVAL query must not be required to span the complete query. Its applicability can be its own supported part; equality with the complete query is not required. Known contradictions still fail even when the opposite endpoint is unknown.

Unknown boundaries are not invented timestamps. Preserve an unresolved episode in the selected inventory with unresolved or qualified temporal support where intersection or coverage cannot be established. Do not remove it merely to produce a supported subset. A known non-overlap contradicts that episode's inclusion in an overlap inventory and must be reported. The owner completeness declaration remains an independently selected assumption, not a completeness theorem. Neither an empty selection nor a successful comparison proves absence in a live inventory.

## B2: observational horizon applies to the complete temporal claim

The independently selected context `fixedAt` is the upper bound on observational support. Require the selected AS_OF point, or the complete finite INTERVAL selection, to be at or before it. Each source applicability and each source assessment/open-as-of time must also be at or before it. Compare at the source's full timestamp precision; do not round away a future nanosecond.

A boundary marked KNOWN asserts an observed boundary. A KNOWN start or end after `fixedAt` must not produce positive observational support, even if the query is earlier. For this repair, reject that contradiction with a specific error. Preserve the submitted bytes in the evidence corpus; do not silently change KNOWN to a forecast or shorten the original interval. A future planned boundary requires a different explicitly qualified source meaning, not an observational shortcut in this interface.

An UNBOUNDED end is a retained source posture, not observed infinity. It must not discharge end coverage or enable an unqualified occupancy claim. Keep it unresolved/qualified, unless a future separately reviewed source meaning actually supplies bounded observational support. An OPEN_AS_OF end may support only the observed range through its stated instant, capped by `fixedAt`; it does not prove a departure at that instant or persistence beyond it. A known lower-bound contradiction must still be checked before returning an unresolved end result. Do not use a large sentinel timestamp as observed support.

## F1: quantities remain qualified even when a cohort is identified

Retain each original decimal, quantity kind, unit, lexical value/unit and qualifier. No arithmetic equivalence, conversion, normalization, summation or replacement with a cohort count is implied. A reported amount with MAXIMUM, MINIMUM, ESTIMATED or UNKNOWN qualification cannot support an unqualified claim of that amount. An identified cohort does not erase a simultaneous quantity qualification.

For the existing broad `selectedPostureSupportsUnqualifiedClaim` indicator, require every reported amount qualification to be EXACT, in addition to the existing temporal, validity, dispute, revision and extent requirements. An explicitly unreported quantity can support only the independently identified cohort branch, with the absence of an amount retained; it supplies no numerical amount. If the owner instead introduces separate amount and cohort indicators, #47 must inspect the indicator corresponding to what its exact allegation says and must preserve both original postures. A bare renamed indicator that silently ignores quantity qualification is insufficient for the existing #47 unqualified-occupancy check.
